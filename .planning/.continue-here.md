## THREE THINGS NEED AN OWNER RULING BEFORE THE WORK THEY BLOCK

These are the only reasons anything is stalled. Each is a decision, not a task.

### 1. Phase 19 criterion 5 — refused, not priced

The criterion reads *"the N-th identity costs more than the first."* What exists is a **bound
made durable**: the issuance budget survives restart, measured across real processes including
two-provider recovery. But the N-th enroller is **refused inside the window, not priced**.

**Measured comparatively, inside one run:**
- provider's cost to refuse ÷ attacker's cost to mint a fresh identity = **3.02, 3.06, 3.01**
- provider's cost to refuse ÷ attacker's cost to replay = **1397×**
- so burning a provider's whole window costs one dialer `budget` fresh identities at roughly a
  third of the CPU the provider spends answering them — and the denial then applies to every
  honest node for the rest of the window **at no further cost**.

**The obvious mitigation was measured and FAILS.** A capacity slot on the enrol branch served
**8 of 8** concurrent enrolments, because `enrol` is synchronous so the bound never binds; in a
rig where an `exec` held the shared table it served 0 of 8. It bounds the wrong verb.

Two mutually exclusive routes, and `19-VERIFICATION.md` says the second **may not be taken
silently**: (1) build a per-identity cost the N-th enroller pays and the first does not, and
measure both; or (2) amend criterion 5's second clause to the bound-made-durable reading,
recorded in `ROADMAP.md` the way criterion 1's rewording was.

Your 2026-08-02 ruling — *"even with relatively long expiration period for certs, the attack
radius is waaay too small"* — points at route 2, since N sybils under one operator buy exactly
one quorum slot. But amending a criterion is an owner act, and *descoped is not satisfied*.
