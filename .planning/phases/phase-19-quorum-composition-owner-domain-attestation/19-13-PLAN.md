# Phase 19 · Plan 13 — The third leg: a result a node signed

**Closes:** nothing on its own. Builds the leg 19-14 carries and 19-15 wires, without which
`attestationReceipt` is the submitter's word about itself.

<objective>
**Two of the three signing legs already exist; this is the missing one.** Owner decision
2026-08-02, recorded in `19-CONTEXT.md` under *"The signing triangle"*:

| leg | signer | verified against | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **code** a node runs | publisher | pinned `trustAnchors` (`naming.ts:99-103`), through `guardModuleProvenance` | exists, Phase 14 |
| the **node certificate** | provider | `verifyCertificate` vs `trustedIssuers` (`enrollment.ts:340-352`) | exists, Phase 17 |
| the **result** | the node, with its certified `nodeKey` | the `nodeKey` inside its own provider-signed certificate | **this plan** |

**Why it is not cosmetic.** Agreement is attested today by **transport authentication only**.
Noise proves peer X sent this frame, but that proof is **not transferable** — the submitter
cannot show it to anyone. `VerificationResult`'s `agreed` arm carries
`agreeing: readonly string[]`, plain node-id **strings** (`verify.ts:85-95`), and a receipt
built on that is worth exactly the submitter's word about itself. That is not what VER-08,
VER-09 and VER-10 promise.

With the third leg, a third party holding **only the provider's public key** can check: *this
output came from a node that provider enrolled, running code that provider published.* That is
what turns `attestationReceipt` into a transferable attestation.

**The leg covers two verbs, and this plan builds the challenge for both.** Owner decision
2026-08-02: signing `exec` alone would leave a map/reduce job ending with signed map results
feeding an **unsigned aggregation** — precisely the half `PROJECT.md` calls the verified one. So
`result-attestation.ts` carries **two** challenges from the start, and Plan 19-16 consumes the
combine one. Building both here costs a few lines and keeps one file the authority on what this
fabric signs; building the second later would have meant two modules with two encodings to keep
canonical, which is the failure `possessionChallenge`'s shape exists to prevent.

**This plan builds it and wires none of it, deliberately.** The wrapper is composed nowhere and
the attestation is read nowhere; every executor in the tree reports the unsigned sentinel. That
is a **stated scheduled arrival**, not an oversight: 19-14 makes `agreeing` carry it, 19-15
composes the wrapper at the two production factories. Splitting it this way is what keeps each
plan's mechanical fan-out under one context window — **41 `{ok:true,…,fuelUsed}` construction
sites across 20 files** here, and about 30 `agreeing` sites across 11 files in 19-14, both
counted on 2026-08-02 rather than estimated.

Output: a signature a stranger can check, a wrapper that produces it, and a wire that carries
it.
</objective>

## Recorded decisions

- **The challenge covers work, answer and signer, and nothing time-varying.** An attestation is
  therefore valid forever for that triple, which is what makes it transferable. Freshness, if
  ever needed, is the certificate's `expiresAt`.
- **`partitionCount` is out of the challenge.** It is a property of the job, not of the unit of
  work; including it would make one shard signed differently in two jobs over the same
  partition.
- **The attestation carries the whole certificate.** The owner's goal is a third party holding
  only the provider's public key; a bare node key would need an out-of-band lookup that reader
  does not have.
- **`nodeKey` is not repeated beside the certificate.** Two copies of one value is a
  disagreement waiting to be introduced.
- **A malformed attestation refuses the frame.** Degrading to the sentinel would report a
  protocol error as an unenrolled peer and quietly weaken the receipt.
- **The kernel executors do not sign.** A signing kernel needs an identity, which is what
  `ports.ts` exists to keep out. Signing is a wrapper at a node's construction, like leg 1.
- **A signature is not a substitute for redundancy.** It says a certified node computed this;
  it says nothing about whether the answer is right. Somebody will propose reducing redundancy
  because results are signed now; the module says why that is wrong.
