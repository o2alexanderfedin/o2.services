# Phase 18 · Plan 06 — A job placed from a CID, across real processes

> **Numbers in this plan are configuration choices, not derived claims. Any quantity
> describing runtime behaviour must be measured before it is written down anywhere,
> including in a source comment.**

**Closes:** criterion 1 and criterion 2.

<objective>
Everything this needs now exists and nothing calls it. Plan 18-03 made every node answer
`providers` from its own store; Plan 18-02 made `RpcRecordIndex` union those answers; Plan
18-05 built `discoverCandidates` and gave `submitJob` an offer arm; Plan 18-01 gave
`bin/agent.ts` a flag that dials a peer and one that sets a node's slot limit. This plan
puts them together against real processes and gives the new capability a call path from a
runnable entry point.

**The entry point matters as much as the test.** Phase 15 proposed accepting a capability
as entry-point-unreachable and was overruled on 2026-07-31 — *"shipping an adapter with no
callers is the defect this milestone exists to remove, and naming it is not the same as
fixing it"* (`ROADMAP.md`, Phase 22). Phase 22's guard fails on exactly that, over five
entry points. So `discoverCandidates` gets one here, and it gets the shape the roadmap
already blessed for the sovereign leg it sent to Phase 23: **opt-in, off by default,
default curve unmoved**, so 15-CONTEXT.md decision 2 — a published scaling curve must not
be reshaped by an undeclared change — survives.

Two things this plan must not paper over, both stated in the tests themselves. The
requestor still has to know **addresses** to dial; what discovery removes is the
**executor list**, and the test makes that difference visible by dialling nodes that turn
out not to qualify. And the reach is **directly-connected peers only** — no transitive
routing, no DHT — which is the honest limit of what criterion 1 proves.
</objective>
