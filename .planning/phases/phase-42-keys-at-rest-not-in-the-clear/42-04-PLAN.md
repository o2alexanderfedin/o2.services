<objective>
Where the passphrase actually comes from for the person the fabric is asking to donate a
browser tab. Criterion 3 presupposes *"the correct passphrase"*; this is the plan that
makes one exist without asking a stranger for a password before they have anything to lose.

Purpose: criteria 3 and 4 read off the surface a visitor really uses, in a real tab, rather
than off an options type.

Output: a passphrase set at the moment of enrolment, an unlock on return, a refusal that
names itself in the page, and one explicit escape hatch that states its own cost.
</objective>

## Phase Goal

**As a** volunteer whose device can be lost, seized or imaged, **I want to** have every
long-lived secret this fabric writes to my device be unreadable without my passphrase,
**so that** losing the device does not also hand over the identity that speaks for me on
the fabric.

This plan's slice: the visitor is asked, once, at the only moment the question makes sense
to them.

## The answer, and why the two obvious ones lose

**A volunteer opening a web page has no passphrase and will not enjoy being asked for one.**
This cohort is spendable exactly once — a few hundred people in a Telegram group — so a
prompt that costs conversion costs it permanently. Three shapes were considered.

**Loser 1 — prompt on first use and on every new session.** Honest, and it satisfies the
goal sentence for everybody. It also puts a password field between a stranger and the first
thing they came to see, before the page has given them any reason to care. Rejected on cost,
and the cost is not recoverable.

**Loser 2 — a device-held key wrapping the seed** (a non-extractable WebCrypto key in
IndexedDB, no passphrase). No prompt at all, and it genuinely defeats **a copy of the
identity database**: `exportKey` throws, and the database cannot be opened from another
origin. **It does not defeat an imaged device**, because the wrapping key lives in the same
browser profile as the ciphertext and unwraps anything the image contains. The goal sentence
is *"unreadable without my passphrase, so that losing the device does not also hand over the
identity"* — and a mechanism that a lost device hands over does not meet it, whatever else it
is good for. It is rejected here **and it is not described in criterion 1's language
anywhere in this phase**; whether a profile image actually yields such a key is unmeasured
and `42-05` task 2 measures it.

**The answer taken — ask at the moment there is something to protect.** A tab that has not
enrolled holds no certificate, and its seed is worth what a fresh one is worth: nothing.
It runs under `writes-no-new-secret`, writes no secret to the device at all, and is never
asked anything. **Enrolment is the moment that changes**: a provider is about to sign a
certificate naming this key, the key acquires a value it did not have, and the visitor is
already inside a deliberate, consented act with a surface of its own. The passphrase field
goes in that surface, beside the decision it belongs to.

Three properties follow, and each is a case in task 1:

- **Nobody is asked before they have enrolled.** No prompt on a cold visit.
- **A returning enrolled visitor is asked to unlock**, once, at start. Correct → same node.
- **Wrong → a refusal that names itself**, and never a fresh identity. A silent re-mint
  presents as success, burns the enrolment quota under the issuance budget, and orphans the
  certificate the old seed was issued against.

## The escape hatch, and why it must exist and must be explicit

A visitor who forgets the passphrase would otherwise face a page they can never use again.
So there is a way out — and it is **an explicit act with its cost written on it**, never a
fallback the code takes on their behalf. The surface says, in plain words, that continuing
means becoming a different node, needing to enrol again, and abandoning the certificate the
old key holds. Only after that act does the page start under `writes-no-new-secret` with a
new identity.

The difference between this and the defect criterion 4 forbids is not the outcome — both end
with a different node. It is **who decided, and whether they were told.**

## The node tier's answer is different, and deliberately

An operator running `bin/agent.ts` can reasonably be asked for a passphrase, has a
filesystem to keep it in, and is not a conversion-rate problem. `42-02` gives them
`--identity-passphrase-file` and an `O2_IDENTITY_PASSPHRASE` fallback, and no flag that
takes a literal, because argv is world-readable through `ps`. Two tiers, two answers, one
mechanism underneath.

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| visitor keystrokes → page memory | The passphrase, for the session. |
| page → funnel reporter | Telemetry leaves the device. The passphrase must never approach this boundary. |
| page → identity database | Where the sealed record and the salt live. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-42-22 | Information disclosure | the passphrase leaking into `localStorage`, a log, a URL or the funnel | mitigate | Held in a module-scoped variable only; asserted by grep, and the funnel's frozen digest would redden on a schema change independently. |
| T-42-23 | Spoofing | a mistyped passphrase silently producing a new node | mitigate | The refusal state is the only outcome; the escape hatch is a separate act reachable only from it. Both planted red. |
| T-42-24 | Denial of service | a visitor locked out of a page forever by a forgotten passphrase | mitigate | The escape hatch exists. Its cost is written on it, so taking it is a decision rather than an accident. |
| T-42-25 | Information disclosure | a shoulder-surfed passphrase field | accept | A password field on a page the visitor chose to use; out of this fabric's control and not made worse by anything here. |
| T-42-26 | Tampering | a same-origin script reading the passphrase out of page memory | accept | A same-origin script already holds the running node's raw seed, because libp2p needs it in memory for Noise. Nothing at this layer can be stronger than that, and §3.9 names the gap: *"Derivation moves the risk from at-rest to at-use — the enclave closes that gap."* A tab has no enclave. |
</threat_model>
