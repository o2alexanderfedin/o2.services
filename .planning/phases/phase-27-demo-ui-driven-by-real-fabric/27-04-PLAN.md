<objective>
Wire the colouring surface — all 21 regions of UI-SPEC section 4.2 — and land **P5**,
the liveness property.

P5 is the reason this plan exists as a peer of the guard rather than as one more screen.
UI-SPEC states it plainly: *P2, P3 and P4 are all satisfiable by a page that renders
nothing. A guard that can only fail in one direction is half a guard.* So this plan
supplies the direction the others cannot: after a real two-tab run, a surface's reading
regions must no longer equal their absence text.

It is written **generically — over every surface that has a primary run control** —
because π and bring-your-own land after it and must inherit it without an edit. At this
plan's own landing there is exactly one such control, `#run`, and one such surface.

Colouring goes first among the surfaces because it is the one that already works. The
shipped page genuinely runs: driven against a real relay it consented, joined, reached
`2 node(s) computing`, found a colouring at n = 300, 400 and 500, reported `no answer` at
600 at a verification cost of 2.00x, and the local verifier re-derived 386 triples and
accepted. Nothing here is a rescue. What changes is that the result stops being one
`<pre>` and becomes 21 declared regions — **with the `<pre>` unchanged, line for line**,
because `attestation-ui.e2e.test.ts` reads eleven substrings out of it and
`colouring-demo.e2e.test.ts` four more.

Purpose: prove the region mechanism against the workload that already has a reading, and
close the one hole the anti-placeholder guard cannot close by itself.
Output: `surfaces/colouring.ts`, the colouring panel's 21 regions, `WIRED_SURFACES`
gaining `'colouring'`, and `demo-liveness.e2e.test.ts`.
</objective>
