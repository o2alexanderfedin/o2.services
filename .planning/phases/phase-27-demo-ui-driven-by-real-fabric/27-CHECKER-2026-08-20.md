## 4 · Typography — DOES NOT PASS as §1.3 is written

§1.3 opens *"Four sizes, two weights"* and gives Display `clamp(24px, 4vw, 32px)`, Heading `20px`,
Body `15px`, Small `12px`. The rendered page carries **eight sizes and three weights**.

**The finding is narrower and more interesting than that count.** All four roles *exist* in
`demo.css` and two of them are correct: `h2` is `clamp(24px, 4vw, 32px)` — the Display size,
resolving to **32px** at 1440 — and `h3` is `20px`, the Heading size. What diverges is three
specific things:

| what | where | §1.3 says |
|---|---|---|
| **`.card-title` overrides `h3` down to `17px`** (`demo.css:288`) | card titles, the exact role Heading was written for | Heading `20px`, *"Used for: Card titles, panel headings"* |
| `h1 { clamp(22px, 3.2vw, 28px) }` (`:176`) → renders **28px** | the masthead | not in the table — a fifth role |
| `h4 { 17px }` (`:179`), `#gate dt { 17px }` (`:467`) | sub-headings, the consent gate | not in the table — a sixth role |

**And a third weight is in use.** Table headers render `11px / 700`; §1.3 puts table headers at Small
`12px / 400` and names only two weights. Kickers render **11px** against Small's `12px`, and
`.card-body` prose renders **14px** against Body's `15px`.

**The mechanism behind the whole family is one line**: `body` is `15px` against a `16px` root, and
components size in `em` off it — `0.75em → 11.25 → 11`, `0.95em → 14.25 → 14`. So the page is
internally consistent and every one of those runs passes contrast; what it is not is the table.

**This is a contract-versus-implementation divergence and the checker does not get to resolve it.**
Either the page moves to the four sizes, or §1.3 is amended to describe the em-relative scale that
shipped and to say why. **Decider: owner.**

## 5 · Spacing — PARTIAL

**The ramp is exactly right.** `--space-1…8` read `4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32px` off the live page —
§1.2's re-base off the mockup's fractional `3.4px` base is in and correct.

**Nine padding/gap values in use do not come from it**, and five are fractional — which is the
condition §1.2 adopted the re-base to eliminate:

| value | count | owner |
|---|:--:|---|
| `6px` | 22 | `.btn` row/column gap, `.seg-opt` |
| `10px` | 12 | the nav buttons' vertical padding |
| `14.4px` | 6 | `.btn` inline padding (`0.96em` of a 15px body) |
| `9px` | 4 | `.seg-opt` vertical padding |
| `4.55` · `4.2` · `1.3` · `1.2px` | 16 | `<code>` inline and block padding, em-derived off an already-reduced size |
| `2px` | 2 | `#surfaces` nav gap |

**The cause is not the ramp — it is that these components never consult it.** They size padding in
`em`, so the fractional accumulation §1.2 argued against comes back in through component CSS rather
than through the scale. **And `10px` is a borrowed exception**: §1.4 grants `10px` to *the bar's*
vertical padding by name, and the nav buttons use it without being granted it.

**Decider: owner** — same choice as dimension 4, and probably the same answer: adopt the ramp in
those components, or name these as further exceptions in §1.4 the way the bar and the Stop control
are already named.

# AMENDMENT, same day — **the measurements stand; two of the explanations were invented**

Written hours after the pass, when the owner ruled *amend the contract to the page* and the
amendment had to state what the page actually does. Reading `demo.css` to write it falsified the
mechanism this report gave for both open dimensions.

**What was wrong.** Dimensions 4 and 5 both said the cause was *"`body` is `15px` and components
size in `em` off it"*. They do not.

| this report said | `demo.css` says |
|---|---|
| sizes derive from a 15px body by `em` | **every size is explicit `px`** — nine of them, two clamps. `0.75em → 11.25 → 11` was arithmetic that fit and was never checked against a declaration |
| `.btn`'s `14.4px` is `0.96em` of 15 | **`calc(var(--space-3) * 1.2)`** — the ramp, deliberately scaled. It is *on* the ramp, not off it |
| `.seg-opt`, the nav and `.btn` "size padding in `em`" | all explicit `px`, chosen to land each control on §7.3's `40px`/`44px` floor. **The only `em` on the page is inline `<code>`'s `.1em .35em`**, and that one is correct — a code chip should track the type it wraps |
| table headers at `700` are a third declared weight | **the stylesheet declares exactly two.** `700` is the user agent's own `th { font-weight: bold }`, never overridden |

**The readings themselves are unaffected and were not re-taken.** 11px, 14px, 17px, 14.4px, 4.55px
and `700` are all still what the page renders — the numbers were measured and they were right. What
was wrong was the *story* attached to them, and it was wrong in the way this repository names: **a
number that agrees with a theory is not the theory's proof.** `0.75 × 15 = 11.25` is true and had
nothing to do with why the page says `11px`.

**How it was caught, and it was not by re-measuring.** It was caught by having to *write the
contract down*. Describing the em scale required naming the em declarations, and there were none.
A finding that survives measurement can still die on being made specific.

**And the correction improves the ruling rather than undermining it.** An accidental em cascade
would be a weak thing to adopt as a contract. Nine deliberately-declared steps, with control
padding sized to an accessibility floor, is a scale worth writing down — which is what §1.3 and
§1.4 now carry.

**The last row above changes a verdict rather than a story.** `.table th` at `700` is not the page
disagreeing with its contract; it is the page disagreeing with **its own stylesheet**, which asks
for no such thing. It is therefore the one genuine defect this pass found, it is one line, and it is
carried out of the §12 sign-off deliberately instead of being documented into correctness.

_Amended 2026-08-20 · the measurements held, the mechanism did not_
