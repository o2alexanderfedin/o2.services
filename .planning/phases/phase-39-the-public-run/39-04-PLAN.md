## Phase Goal

**As a** project owner, **I want to** send the first invite only after seven conditions hold on a dated
checklist with named evidence for each, and to send invites in stages by region and by cohort slice with
the funnel read between them, **so that** the fabric's headline claim is finally measured on hundreds of
independently-owned devices rather than asserted.

<objective>
Criterion 4: *"a map/reduce job distributes across nodes on independently-owned devices across several
continents, each machine read off its own announced handshake line rather than off the driver, and the
distinct-machine count is published beside the curve. **Until the run reports, the half stays descoped and
unmeasured — not met — and a same-host figure may not be published in its place.**"*

Three facts settle what this plan can and cannot build.

1. **The announced-handshake mechanism exists, and it is the Node process-spawn driver's.**
   `packages/node/src/bench-inventory.ts` merges each spawned agent's own `AnnouncedMachine` with the
   driver's reading, and `packages/bench/src/report.ts`'s `machineLabel` derives `SAME-MACHINE` from that
   inventory rather than declaring it. The public run is browser tabs, which have no such handshake.
2. **Nothing machine-scoped is announced on the browser job path.** `ReduceOutcome.executedBy` is peer ids
   (`packages/core/src/reduce.ts:398`), and `packages/net/src/agent.ts`'s `AgentOptions` carries no machine
   field. Two tabs on one laptop are two peer ids — which is exactly the over-count `BENCH-06` exists to
   forbid: *"sixteen nodes on one laptop are reported as sixteen processes on one machine."*
3. **Adding one costs a disclosure version.** `packages/browser/src/browser-id.ts` refuses `platform` and
   `hardwareConcurrency` by name, because `DISCLOSURE` promises *"no identifiers beyond a peer key generated
   in this tab"* (`packages/browser/src/disclosure.ts:241`). Announcing a machine datum exceeds that promise,
   which means a version bump, which re-asks a cohort that is spendable exactly once. **That is a privacy
   ruling and it is the owner's** — it is plan 39-07's first row, and it is deliberately sequenced before the
   release cut, because that cut re-asks everyone once anyway.

So this plan builds the half that needs no ruling — a distinct **peer** count, labelled as peers — and the
mechanism that refuses to let it be published as a machine count.

Purpose: make the measurement possible later without letting a same-host figure be published in its place.
Output: `participantLabel`, a publication guard with its own positive control, and a dated statement of what
the missing half waits on.
</objective>
