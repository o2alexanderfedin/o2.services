<objective>
Build the DATA-09 serving-side gate as a pure `Executor` adapter — `guardSovereignty` —
and prove, at the `submitJob` level, that the two mechanisms Plan 12-01 wired
(placement narrowing and per-shard degradation) actually discriminate under load
pressure and actually reject malformed input, rather than merely compiling.

CONTEXT.md is explicit that `Task` carries the label "because the refusal happens at
the far end," and that this is deliberately not the same mechanism as AUTH-03
capability chains (Phase 15): "the DATA-09 refusal is about what this node may
decrypt, not about who authorised the caller." A capability chain proves the
*caller* is allowed to ask; `guardSovereignty` proves the *node* is allowed to
answer. Per Phase 2's standing rule — "if it can be an adapter behind an existing
port, it must be" — this is built as an `Executor` decorator (the same shape as the
existing `GovernedExecutor`), so the `Executor` port itself never changes and
`serveAgent`/`AgentOptions` need no new hook: whoever constructs a serving node's
executor wraps it with `guardSovereignty` before handing it to `serveAgent`.

Purpose: turn `NodeDescriptor.canExecuteSovereign` — "where DATA-09 already lives,"
per CONTEXT.md — into an enforced refusal at the one place that sees a `Task` and
nothing else: the serving node.
Output: a portable, unit-tested gate ready to be wrapped around a real serving
node's executor (Plan 12-04 does that over a real RPC transport), plus the two
`submitJob`-level proofs criteria 1 and 2 ask for.
</objective>
