<objective>
Settle, by measurement rather than by argument, whether closing a relay-capable enrolment
provider breaks enrolment.

`24-VERIFICATION.md` found that a joiner the gate refused still reserved on **the enrolment
provider it had to dial in order to get certified**, and that the same happened to the
in-process `reader` — which holds no certificate and enrols with nobody. That produces an
apparent bootstrap paradox: if every relay-capable peer refuses uncertificated reservations,
how does anyone ever enrol?

**There is a strong hypothesis that the paradox is false**, and this plan tests it rather than
assuming it either way. `--provider-addr` forces a **direct dial**; a direct dial needs no
circuit reservation; and the reservation the refused joiner obtained looks like an automatic
side-effect of libp2p's `RelayDiscovery` firing identify at any HOP-speaking peer, not
something the enrolment RPC needed.

**Nothing in plans 24-06, 24-07 or 24-08 may proceed until this plan has published a verdict.**

Purpose: the three plans after this one all assume enrolment survives a closed provider. If
that assumption is false they are wrong in their design, not in their execution, and this is
the cheapest place to find out.

Output: `packages/node/src/enrolment-needs-no-reservation.node.test.ts` — two arms across real
operating-system processes, plus the mechanism reading that explains `reader`.
</objective>

`packages/node/src/fabric-node.ts` — the two facts this plan's hypothesis rests on, both
already in the tree and neither yet measured through the production argv path:

  - `resolveCertificate` enrols by `await libp2p.dial(multiaddr(enrollment.providerAddr))`
    followed by an RPC over that connection (fabric-node.ts around line 1068). There is no
    reservation anywhere in that path.
  - The relay-dial loop that runs beside it (around line 1892) records **dial** failures into
    `relayFailures` and nothing else. A refused *reservation* is reported through
    `ReservationWatcher` and does not fail `FabricNode.start`.
  - `transports: viaRelay ? [tcp(), webSockets(), circuitRelayTransport()] : [tcp(), webSockets()]`
    where `viaRelay` is `relayAddrs.length > 0`. **A node given no `relayAddrs` installs no
    circuit-relay transport and therefore cannot take a reservation anywhere.** This is what
    makes case 3 below a control rather than decoration.

`packages/node/src/bin/agent.ts` — the argv surface this plan drives:

  - `--admit-issuer <hex>` repeatable, hex-validated, exit 2 on garbage; reaches
    `relayAdmission` through a ternary whose absent arm is the open literal.
  - `--provider-addr <multiaddr> --user-key <path> --operator-id <id>` — enrolment.
  - `--relay-addr <multiaddr>` repeatable — the relays this node asks for a reservation.
  - `--peer-addr <multiaddr>` repeatable — peers this process dials once it is up.
  - The one-line JSON handshake on stdout carries `peerId`, `multiaddrs`, `relayAdmission`
    (posture, `'admits-any-peer'` or a sorted array), `certificate`, `issuerKey`, `relays`,
    `pid`, `nodeKey`.

`packages/net` — the RPC frames the reader uses:
  `encodeRequest({ kind: 'reservations' })` and `parseResponse(body)` yielding
  `{ kind: 'reservations', peerIds }`.

`enrol-through-a-closed-door.node.test.ts` already holds the **in-process** half of this
reading, green: *"refuses the reservation, and the same peer still connects, starts, serves

never been measured is the same claim **through `bin/agent.ts --provider-addr` against a
provider closed by `--admit-issuer`**, which is the deployment the next three plans assume.
